Art Basel Paris is a contemporary art fair held each October at the Grand Palais. The 2026 edition, its fifth, brings together 211 exhibitors from 41 countries and territories, and opens to the public from 23 to 25 October.

## What the fair is

At a fair, galleries rather than museums are the exhibitors. Each gallery takes a booth and shows work by the artists it represents, and the work is for sale. For a visitor that makes it an unusually wide survey: dozens of programmes, many countries and several generations of artists in one building, in a few hours.

Art Basel Paris began in 2022, then under the name Paris+ par Art Basel, held in the temporary Grand Palais Éphémère near the Eiffel Tower while the Grand Palais was being restored. It succeeded the long-running FIAC. The 2024 edition was the first in the restored Grand Palais, and in 2025 the fair occupied the whole building for the first time.

In 2026 the fair is directed by Karim Crippa, who describes this edition as shaped by and for Paris.

![The fair floor seen from a balcony beneath the green iron roof of the Grand Palais, visitors moving between booths](/images/event-art-basel-paris-2025-hall.jpg)

## How it is organised

The fair has three sectors.

- **Galeries** is where galleries present their programmes.
- **Emergence** is on the balcony level. In 2025 it held 16 gallery projects, mostly from galleries taking part for the first time, and is where to look for what comes next.
- **Premise**, created in 2024, shows works made before 1900 within a contemporary context, placing older and newer work side by side.

## What is new in 2026

- Nearly 30 galleries are taking part for the first time.
- A record 12 joint booths, where galleries share a space, which the fair describes as a growing culture of collaboration.
- More than 60 exhibitors operate spaces in France.
- **Oh La La!**, the fair's rehang initiative, returns on 23 and 24 October. After the preview days, selected booths are rehung with fresh installations; this year it is led by a guest collaborator from outside the art world.

Alongside the fair runs its Public Program, now in its fifth edition, developed with leading Parisian institutions. Miu Miu is the fair's official partner. In 2025 the programme reached beyond the Grand Palais to places including the Institut de France, the Hôtel de la Marine and Place Vendôme, so it is worth checking the 2026 programme for what is open that week.

## The dates

![The glass dome of the Grand Palais at night, a French flag above the roofline](/images/destination-paris-grand-palais-night.jpg)

According to the fair:

- **20 October** — Avant-Première, an invitation-only preview, now in its second edition.
- **21 and 22 October** — Preview Days.
- **22 October** — the public vernissage, by ticket or VIP pass.
- **23 to 25 October** — public days.

The Grand Palais is at 3 Avenue du Général Eisenhower, in the 8th arrondissement. The nearest metro station is Champs-Élysées Clemenceau. Tickets for the public days are sold through the fair's official site, so it is worth buying them before you travel.

## How we would visit it

You do not need to see everything, and the fair is large enough that trying to is the usual mistake. A few ways to give a visit shape:

- **Start with a question rather than a map.** Emergence for what is new, Premise for the unexpected pairing of old and new, Galeries for the established programmes. Pick one to begin with and let the rest follow.
- **Split the visit.** One focused outing, then a slower return, usually shows more than one long day.
- **Talk to the galleries.** Gallery teams are there to explain the work. Quieter hours are better for this than the busiest ones.
- **Leave room for the city.** The wider week is full of openings and institutions that sit well alongside a morning at the fair.

## If you are travelling for it

If you are coming to Paris for the week, the Golden Triangle and the streets towards Place de la Concorde are the natural bases, close to the Grand Palais. ## Frequently asked questions

**Is the fair open to the public?** Yes, from 23 to 25 October, with a ticket. The earlier days are for preview and invited visitors.

**What is the Avant-Première?** An invitation-only preview on 20 October, for galleries and their principal clients.

**Can I buy work at the fair?** The work shown is for sale by the exhibiting galleries. Ask the gallery about a work and its price.

**Do I need to know about contemporary art?** No. The fair is a good place to start precisely because so many programmes sit under one roof, and gallery teams are used to questions.
