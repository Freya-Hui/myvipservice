Most people assume the hardest part of shopping in Milan is picking which brand to visit. In practice, what actually wears people out is everything around the shopping itself — queuing, communication, timing that doesn't line up, and not knowing how to get it all home once you've bought too much.

## When to avoid

Milan Fashion Week runs roughly late February and late September each year — city-centre hotels get tight, and some boutiques shorten their hours for regular customers or close temporarily for events. If the goal is a calm shopping trip rather than catching the shows, we'd suggest avoiding these two windows — boutique appointments are also easier to secure at a good time outside them.

## An appointment beats queuing

Boutiques on the fashion streets often have real queues during high season; a private appointment skips that entirely, with the boutique ready for you at your arrival time rather than waiting at the door. Language isn't a barrier either — the shopping appointments we arrange can run in Mandarin, English or French — but what actually determines how efficiently a visit goes is whether you've told us in advance what kind of pieces you're after: focused on a handful of specific brands, or wanting a stylist to put together looks for an occasion. The route we build looks completely different depending on which one it is.

## Know the tax refund process ahead of time

As a non-EU resident, spending above a certain amount in Italy qualifies for a VAT refund (commonly known as detax) — you'll need to request the refund form at the boutique when you buy, and get it stamped at customs before leaving the EU, after which the refund is processed at the airport or online. Airport refund counters can have long queues during peak season, so it's worth building in at least an extra hour rather than leaving it to the last minute before boarding.

## Sizing runs differently from what you're used to

Italian brands generally use European sizing, and shoe and clothing sizes don't map directly onto domestic conventions — sizing can even vary between brands for the same nominal size. If there's something specific you already want to buy, telling us the item and your usual size ahead of time lets the boutique have the right size ready to try, which saves time versus measuring on the spot.

## Itinerary tips

- **The driver waits between boutiques, so you're not carrying your own shopping bags.** Buying a fair amount over the course of a day is normal — the driver we arrange waits between each boutique and handles the transfers, so you're not lugging bags to the next stop yourself.
- **Don't try to fit every brand into a single day.** If your list is long, splitting it across two half-days with a lunch break in between makes for a much more comfortable experience than cramming it all in at once.

## Frequently asked questions

**When does the tax refund money typically arrive?** A cash refund at the counter is usually paid on the spot; a refund to your credit card generally takes a few weeks to a month or two, depending on how the refund provider processes it.

**How far ahead should a boutique appointment be booked?** At least 1–2 weeks as a baseline, and earlier for in-demand brands or dates near Fashion Week.

**Can private appointments also be arranged for bags and jewellery, not just clothing?** Yes — let us know the brands and categories you're interested in and we'll confirm whether that boutique supports private appointments.
