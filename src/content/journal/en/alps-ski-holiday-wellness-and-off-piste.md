Not everyone in a travelling group wants to ski all day — some want to chase off-piste terrain, others would rather sleep in and spend a morning on wellness. Rather than compromising to accommodate one side, it's often better to build both into the same trip.

## Choosing when in the season to go

The French Alps ski season runs roughly December through April. January and February have the most reliable snow conditions, but also the coldest temperatures and earliest dark — by 4pm there's often little skiable light left. March brings warmer temperatures and longer daylight, generally a more comfortable experience, though spring snow softens and some off-piste routes shift condition with temperature — which is exactly why off-piste routes are confirmed the morning of, not locked in days ahead. Christmas–New Year and the mid-February French school holidays are the two most crowded windows of the year, with noticeably longer lift queues and harder restaurant reservations — if the dates are flexible, avoiding these two stretches makes for a noticeably better experience.

## Off-piste skiing: fitness and gear preparation

Off-piste demands meaningfully more leg strength and cardio than skiing within marked runs — routes are longer, snow conditions less even, and there's essentially no lift access mid-route. If you don't have a regular exercise habit, we'd suggest starting some baseline conditioning (brisk walking, squats, cycling all work) 3–4 weeks ahead of departure — it makes a noticeable difference on the day.

Off-piste days are led by a genuinely certified guide, not a standard resort lesson — routes are set according to your actual ability level, with avalanche safety equipment (beacon, probe, shovel) provided as part of a private itinerary. The guide confirms how to use the equipment before setting off, so there's no need to learn it beforehand. The fitness and skill requirements are real, and we'll confirm your ability level with you in advance to avoid setting a mismatched route.

## How to plan wellness time

After several consecutive days of skiing, muscles and the body need to recover. A mountain wellness morning is built to be unhurried — not about the number of things you do, but genuine rest — and we generally suggest scheduling it after an off-piste day rather than before, so the body works first and recovers second, which tends to work better than resting before any exertion.

The two can alternate within the same trip rather than being an either/or choice — ski the first two days, build in a recovery wellness day, then decide based on energy whether to add another off-piste day. Lake Geneva isn't far from the Alps, and for a trip outside ski season, we also have a themed journey combining the Alps and Lake Geneva, which makes the shift in pace between mountain and lake more distinct.

## Packing and travel tips

- **Layering works better than a single heavy down jacket** — a moisture-wicking base layer, an insulating mid-layer, and a windproof, waterproof shell handle the temperature swings of active skiing better than one thick coat.
- **Don't forget sunscreen and lip balm** — UV at altitude is stronger than it feels, even on an overcast day.
- **Bring two sets of ski goggle lenses** (one for sun, one for flat light) — mountain weather shifts quickly, and one lens won't handle every light condition.
- **Avoid scheduling intense skiing on arrival day** — give yourself time to adjust to altitude and time zone first; the second day tends to go noticeably better.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can this trip work for someone with no skiing experience at all?** Off-piste requires solid ability on marked runs, so we'd suggest being comfortable on intermediate slopes at minimum before considering it. For beginners, we'd suggest starting with private lessons on marked runs — the wellness and mountain experiences have no skiing prerequisite.

**Is off-piste suitable for children?** Off-piste routes demand a fairly high level of fitness and technical skill, so we generally wouldn't recommend it for younger children — for families, we'd suggest focusing on the warm-season version of this journey (see the related journey below) or private lessons within marked runs.

**Which month has the best snow?** January and February typically have the most reliable, consolidated snow; if daylight and comfort matter more, March is a good middle ground, though it comes with softer spring-condition routes.
