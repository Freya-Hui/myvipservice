Art Basel Paris returns to the Grand Palais this October with 211 exhibitors from 41 countries and territories. The fair itself takes a few days; the week around it is where a trip either runs smoothly or doesn't. This is how we would order it.

![A large blue-and-gold painting hung on a white gallery wall, viewed from a raised walkway](/images/experience-art-gallery.jpg)

## The week at a glance

According to the fair, the dates for 2026 are:

- **20 October** — Avant-Première, by invitation only.
- **21 and 22 October** — Preview Days.
- **22 October** — the public vernissage.
- **23 to 25 October** — public days.

The fair is at the Grand Palais, 3 Avenue du Général Eisenhower, in the 8th arrondissement, and is organised in three sectors: Galeries, Emergence and Premise. Roughly 30 galleries are taking part for the first time, so even regular visitors will find new names.

One point worth being plain about: the Avant-Première is invitation-only, and invitations come from the galleries, not from us or from any concierge. We don't promise access to it. What we can do is make sure the rest of the week works around whichever day you are actually in the building.

## Choose the days first, then everything else

The three groups of days suit different visitors. The preview days are for people who want to buy and to speak with gallery teams while the aisles are still quiet. The public days are livelier and shorter on conversation. If you can, split it: one focused visit, then a slower return.

Whichever you choose, fix the days before you fix the hotel. It determines which neighbourhood makes sense, and what time the car needs to be waiting.

## Where to stay

The fair sits just off the Champs-Élysées, so the Golden Triangle and the streets running down to Place de la Concorde are the natural bases. A few we would look at first:

![The façade of the Hôtel de Crillon on Place de la Concorde](/images/accommodation-hotel-de-crillon.jpg)

**Hôtel de Crillon** is an 18th-century palace on Place de la Concorde, within walking distance of the Tuileries, the Champs-Élysées and Rue Saint-Honoré. A good fit if you want the week to feel like Paris rather than an exhibition hall.

![The façade of Le Meurice on Rue de Rivoli, facing the Tuileries Garden](/images/accommodation-le-meurice.jpg)

**Le Meurice** faces the Tuileries Garden between the Louvre and Place de la Concorde, which puts the museum side of the week within reach as well as the fair.

![The entrance of Maison Villeroy, an eleven-room private-residence-style hotel](/images/accommodation-maison-villeroy-facade.jpg)

**Maison Villeroy** is an eleven-room private-residence-style hotel in the Golden Triangle, an easy walk from Avenue Montaigne, the Champs-Élysées and the Grand Palais. It suits a visitor who would rather come back to somewhere quiet than to a large lobby.

![The façade of La Réserve Paris, a private mansion near the Champs-Élysées](/images/accommodation-la-reserve.jpg)

**La Réserve** is a private mansion of 1854 near the Champs-Élysées, with 40 rooms and suites. Its Imperial Suite looks out over the Grand Palais, among other landmarks.

If you are not sure which kind of hotel suits the trip, our guide to [Palace and boutique hotels in Paris](/en/journal/paris-palace-vs-boutique-hotels-guide/) explains the difference.

## Moving around: where the car matters

Between the fair, dinners and gallery openings, the days tend to be made of short journeys at awkward moments. The Grand Palais is served by the Champs-Élysées Clemenceau metro station, which is fine for a quiet morning but less so for an evening in formal clothes with a purchase to bring home.

Two things are worth settling in advance with a private driver: where you will be dropped off and collected around the Grand Palais at the busiest hours, and how a large work will travel if you buy one. Agree the pick-up point beforehand rather than improvising, and keep the same driver across the days so nothing has to be explained twice. ## Frequently asked questions

**Can you get me into the Avant-Première?** No. Entry is by invitation from the galleries, and we don't promise access. We can plan the rest of the week around it if you have an invitation.

**Do I need a ticket for the public days?** Yes. Admission is by ticket or VIP pass, and tickets are bought through the fair's official site, so it is worth buying them before you travel.

**Which days are best?** It depends on what you want from the fair. Preview days for buying and conversation, public days for atmosphere. We would help you choose once we know your plans.

**How close should the hotel be?** Close enough that going back for a change of clothes between the fair and dinner is easy, which is why we start with the Golden Triangle and the streets towards Place de la Concorde.
