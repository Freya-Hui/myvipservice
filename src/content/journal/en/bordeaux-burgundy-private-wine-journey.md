## This isn't a château tour — it's an entry point into French wine itself

French wine has never been only about what's in the glass. It comes from soil composition, the angle of a hillside, how light and rainfall are distributed across a single year; from the accumulated logic of a château or domaine passed down across generations; from a winemaker's judgment on every individual parcel, and from architecture, table and way of life that have carried on, unchanged in essence, for centuries. When we talk about a bottle of Bordeaux or Burgundy, we're really talking about geography and culture condensed into a single glass.

Which is exactly why a genuine high-end wine journey shouldn't be reduced to "a few châteaux, a few tastings, a car." That treats something layered as if it were a checklist.

MYVIPSERVICE's role is to start from your own wine preferences — the vintages and regions you already gravitate toward, the collection you're building or already hold, how much time you want to give this trip, who's travelling with you, and what you expect from dining and where you stay — and build the entire journey outward from there, rather than fixing a route first and filling it in afterward.

The real value of a trip like this was never "I visited Bordeaux." It's "I got into this world in a way an ordinary visitor simply can't." That depends on three things: **access, expertise, and seamless service** — which is exactly what this piece is about.

## Bordeaux: from château to private cellar

Bordeaux isn't one wine region — it's a collection of quite different ones. The Right Bank's Saint-Émilion runs on limestone soils and Merlot, and its estates are some of the most photogenic in Bordeaux; the Left Bank's Médoc belongs to Cabernet Sauvignon, home to most of the storied names collectors already know by heart. The two banks have distinct characters, and few people try to cover both in a single day — our private château itineraries are usually built around one bank at a time rather than skimming both.

**Private château visits**: we start by understanding what you already drink, which regions interest you, or the direction your collection is heading, and select châteaux worth visiting on that basis — not hand you a standard tourist route. What we arrange can include private estate tours, private cellar visits, exclusive tastings and time in the vineyard itself, depending on what each château can accommodate at the time.

**Tastings designed around your level**: the tasting itself should also be tailored — an introductory tasting for someone just discovering Bordeaux, a horizontal tasting comparing regional styles, a vertical tasting across vintages from a single château, or a technical tasting comparing neighbouring terroirs. We ask about your experience level in advance and shape the stop accordingly.

**Time with the owner or winemaker**: where a château's schedule allows, we try to arrange private time with the owner, a family member, the winemaker or the estate's director — we don't just tell you where to go, we plan who you meet, what you taste and how you get in, and that coordination begins well before the itinerary is finalised.

**Private transport between estates**: there's real driving distance between central Bordeaux and the Médoc or Saint-Émilion. We handle it with a private driver and dedicated vehicle, so you're not tied to a group's schedule or worrying about parking and routes.

## Saint-Émilion: vineyards, heritage and private moments

Saint-Émilion is itself a UNESCO World Heritage town — medieval lanes, limestone buildings, underground cellars carved beneath the hillside, and vineyards running right up to the town walls. But what's actually worth designing here isn't "a walk through the village" — it's turning the town into a full day, or several connected days, with a real narrative arc.

A typical day might unfold like this: a morning tour through the vineyard or château, understanding how that year's harvest and vinification are going; a midday transition into an elevated local lunch, usually near the estate itself; an afternoon underground cellar visit or private tasting; and an evening private dinner echoing the wines tasted earlier that day. What actually defines high-end custom travel isn't cramming several châteaux into one day — it's making each part of the day speak to the next, with real continuity.

We also account for the fact that not everyone in your party is necessarily focused on wine — if you're travelling with children, or someone in your group doesn't drink much, we can weave in château architecture and history, French gastronomy, local craft and countryside walks alongside the tastings. MYVIPSERVICE isn't only in service of "wine experts" — it's in service of everyone travelling with you, each experiencing the trip their own way.

## Burgundy: from terroir to domaine

Burgundy runs on an entirely different logic from Bordeaux. There's no Bordeaux-style grand château here — instead, a large number of mostly small, family-run domaines, and a classification system collectors often spend years genuinely learning to read — from regional appellations up through village level, Premier Cru and Grand Cru, where two neighbouring parcels in the same village can express completely different terroirs and completely different wines.

Because most domaines are family-run with limited capacity for visitors, Burgundy actually depends even more than Bordeaux on genuine private coordination — this isn't "buy a ticket at the gate," it requires reaching out in advance, selecting the right domaines based on your interests, and arranging the appropriate experience within what each estate can accommodate at the time. That's exactly why Burgundy suits private, custom travel so well — its scarcity itself demands advance planning.

## Côte de Nuits: the collector's route

Burgundy's Route des Grands Crus runs roughly north to south. The northern Côte de Nuits is the heartland of Pinot Noir, home to the names collectors already know — Gevrey-Chambertin, Vosne-Romanée, Chambolle-Musigny.

In the Côte de Nuits, we can arrange private domaine visits, vineyard walks, terroir explanations from the estate itself, cellar tours and tastings led by a professional sommelier or wine expert. If you're a collector, we can also build a more targeted route around a specific domaine, a specific vintage, a specific Grand Cru, or even a specific village.

A genuine Burgundy experience was never about covering several sights in a day — it's about entering a world an ordinary visitor can barely access, which takes time and relationships built well in advance.

## Côte de Beaune: wine and the French way of living

Further south is the Côte de Beaune, home to both red wines and Burgundy's most celebrated whites — Meursault, Puligny-Montrachet and Chassagne-Montrachet all sit within this stretch, with the town of Beaune itself as the region's hub, home to the Hospices de Beaune and its iconic glazed-tile roof.

Here, we prefer to fold wine into the broader narrative of French living rather than treat it in isolation: alongside private tastings, we can arrange Michelin dining, food and wine pairings echoing that day's tastings, cultural visits through Beaune's old town, and stays at a boutique hotel or a château itself. Wine here shouldn't just be a supporting act at the table — it should be part of the whole journey's story.

## Two terroirs, two versions of France

Bordeaux × Burgundy — two terroirs, two versions of France.

Bordeaux belongs to the château, the river and the estate — to grandeur and elegance built up over generations. Burgundy belongs to the domaine, the _climat_ and the vineyard itself — to a more intimate, more restrained relationship between people and land. One tells a story of scale and legacy; the other, of precision and scarcity.

From a château to a _climat_. From a glass of Bordeaux to a bottle of Burgundy — these were always two different versions of France, and that's exactly what this journey is meant to help you actually understand.
