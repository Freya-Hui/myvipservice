Vienna moves at a more formal pace than most European capitals, and rewards a trip built around a handful of set pieces — a palace, an opera evening, a proper coffee house — rather than a packed sightseeing schedule.

## Getting There

Vienna International Airport (VIE) connects to the center via the City Airport Train (CAT), a direct 16-minute run to Wien Mitte, or the slightly slower, cheaper S-Bahn. Once in the city, the Ringstrasse — the grand boulevard that replaced the old city walls — circles the Innere Stadt, the historic core, and most of what a first-time visitor wants to see sits inside or just off it.

## The Old Town and the Palaces

The Hofburg, the Habsburgs' former winter residence, sits right in the Innere Stadt and now houses several museums alongside the still-functioning Spanish Riding School. Schönbrunn Palace, the summer residence, is further out but easily reached by U-Bahn and worth the half-day it takes to see the palace and gardens properly. Vienna's coffee house culture — recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage — isn't a tourist add-on so much as a genuine local institution, with a handful of the classic houses (Café Sacher and Café Central among the best known) still run much as they were a century ago.

## Practical Notes

The opera and concert season runs September to June with a summer pause; the Vienna State Opera also famously sells standing-room tickets on the day for a fraction of the seated price, a tradition worth knowing about even for visitors used to booking well ahead. Visiting outside the season means fewer performances but noticeably quieter palaces and museums. Direct rail connections put Salzburg about two and a half hours away and Budapest around two hours forty minutes, making either an easy add-on to a Vienna stay.
