The Riviera is really a string of towns along one coastline, each with a distinct character — which is why "where to base yourself" matters more here than almost anywhere else in France.

## Getting There and Along the Coast

Nice Côte d'Azur Airport (NCE) is France's second-busiest and the natural gateway, roughly 20–30 minutes from central Nice depending on traffic. A regional train line runs along the coast connecting Nice, Antibes, Cannes and Menton, with Monaco in between — useful for a day trip, though most clients traveling with us move by private car, especially with evening plans that don't fit a train timetable.

## Towns and Villages Worth Knowing

Nice has the widest range of hotels and restaurants and the most walkable old town (Vieux Nice). Cannes is quieter outside festival weeks and has a genuine yacht harbor alongside the film-festival reputation. Antibes and its Cap d'Antibes peninsula lean more residential and low-key, popular with clients who want the coast without the see-and-be-seen pace. Inland and up, Èze is a medieval village built into a cliff face with sweeping sea views — best visited early or late in the day, when tour buses have cleared out. Saint-Paul-de-Vence, further inland, is known for the Fondation Maeght modern art museum and a concentration of galleries in a walled hilltop village.

## Practical Notes

July and August are the busiest and most expensive months, with restaurant bookings tightening noticeably; late May, June and September keep the same warm weather with far more availability. Parking in the old towns is limited and often not worth the effort — this is one destination where hiring a driver pays for itself in avoided frustration alone.
